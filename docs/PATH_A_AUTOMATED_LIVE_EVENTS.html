QuizVerse Automated Live Events
on YouTube — explained simply

A game show that runs itself. Videos are created by AI, published to YouTube automatically, winners are picked from the comments, and prizes go out every Sunday — with zero staff involvement after launch.

The idea in one sentence: every day QuizVerse posts short mystery-quiz videos on YouTube; viewers race to guess the answer in the comments; the fastest correct guess wins, the whole community gets pulled into the app to play along, and every Sunday the week's champions win real prizes — and the entire cycle is run by software, not people.

1 Why we built this

The business problem this solves.

Running live quiz events manually is expensive: someone has to script the content, edit the video, upload it, monitor the comments, pick winners fairly, close the event on time, and hand out prizes. Do that daily across two regions (India + USA) and it becomes a full-time job for several people.

Path A removes the people from the loop entirely. The AI content system writes and renders the videos, the publishing system uploads them, the comment system reads every guess and picks winners with timestamps as proof, events close themselves on schedule, and the Sunday prize ceremony calculates itself. Humans only step in to approve budgets and prize catalogues.

2 A week in the life — what actually happens

Follow one mystery from Monday to Sunday. Everything below happens automatically.

📱Mon
Part 1
The tease video goes live on YouTube

The AI writes a 3-part mystery (for example: "I'm a city of seven islands… I never sleep… who am I?"), renders it as a short video with voiceover and visuals, and publishes it to the QuizVerse YouTube channel. The video ends with one line: "First correct guess in the comments WINS."

@priya_quizzes — "Mumbai!!" · 2 minutes after upload
🕵️Mon–Tue
The system reads every comment

Software scans the comment section through YouTube's official API. It understands spelling mistakes and variations ("Mumbaii", "bombay", "its mumbai bro") using smart text-matching, and records who guessed correctly first, with the exact timestamp — a fair, provable winner. It also saves the funniest wrong answers for later.

🔥Wed
Part 2
The hype video — made from the audience's own comments

Part 2 is generated with the real comments baked into the script: it shows the wrong guesses on screen ("Someone said Delhi… not even close 😂"), teases whether anyone has cracked it, and adds two more clues. Viewers see themselves in the video — which is exactly what makes people comment, share, and come back.

Video script: "437 of you guessed… and ONE of you might already be the winner 👀"
🏆Fri
Part 3
The reveal — winner announced by name

Part 3 reveals the answer and congratulates the winner on screen, by their YouTube handle. If nobody guessed it, the video says so ("NOBODY got it — the answer was…"), which drives even more comments the next round. The winner's achievement also counts toward Sunday's prizes.

"And the first correct guess came from @priya_quizzes — just 2 minutes in. Respect. 🏆"
🎮All week
Meanwhile, inside the app

Live quiz events run in the QuizVerse app all week. Every event now ends itself exactly on schedule — a background service checks every single minute, closes finished events, ranks the final leaderboard, and assigns prize tiers. A second, independent safety system double-checks every 5 minutes, so an event can never be left hanging even if one system goes down. When an event closes, a recap video is generated and published automatically.

Quietly, in the background, every single answer any player submits is counted toward the weekly leaderboard, their daily play-streak, and their activity record.

👑Sun
evening
The Sunday ceremony — champions crowned, prizes sent

At 8:00 PM in India and 5:00 PM Eastern in the USA, the system calculates each region's winners across six award categories (see below), queues gift cards for delivery, credits in-game currency bonuses, notifies every winner inside the app, and posts the winners board publicly. Monday morning, a fresh mystery starts the cycle again.

5 Questions a reviewer usually asks

What if two people guess correctly at almost the same time?
YouTube records the exact publish time of every comment. The system always awards the earliest timestamp. There is no tie-breaking by humans and no ambiguity.
Can the system be fooled by spam or joke comments?
Comments that don't look like genuine guesses are filtered out before matching. The matching itself tolerates typos and phrasing ("its mumbai bro" counts) but requires real similarity to the answer — random spam never matches.
What happens if a system goes down mid-week?
Each critical job has two independent runners. Event-closing, for example, is checked every minute by the primary scheduler and every 5 minutes by a separate backup on our cloud infrastructure. All jobs are also safe to re-run — a double-fire can never award a prize twice.
Does this change the existing creator events in the app?
No. The existing in-app creator event flow (Path B) is completely untouched — same screens, same behaviour. Path A only adds automation around it.
Who controls the prize budget?
Prize catalogues and values are configuration, reviewed and approved by you. The system queues gift-card deliveries through the existing fulfillment pipeline — it never spends outside the approved catalogue.
Is this actually built, or is it a proposal?
It is built and running in production now. The event auto-closing service has been live and verified (running green every minute), the Sunday ceremony is scheduled, and the video/comment pipeline is deployed. One external key is pending — see the sign-off section below.
